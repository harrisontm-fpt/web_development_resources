# Media Student Showcase — Design Brief

## Purpose and message

The website should communicate: **these are our students, this is what they can create, and this is how to contact us about working with them.** Industry visitors should quickly understand the work and identify the people and skills behind it. Lecturers should be able to maintain the showcase confidently.

Use this document alongside the [project brief](media_student_showcase_project.md). It provides design direction; students create their own wireframes, mock-ups, and page layouts.

## Branding and visual style

- Use the relevant university or department name, approved logo, and brand guidance when supplied. The exact branding, colours, and fonts remain to be confirmed by the client.
- As a proposed starting point, use neutral backgrounds, dark readable text, one restrained accent colour, and a legible typeface. Let the student work provide most of the colour and visual interest.
- Give project images space and establish a clear hierarchy between titles, student names, descriptions, and actions.
- Keep navigation and controls consistent. If featured content is implemented, distinguish it clearly and explain the lecturer's selection.
- Avoid decorative effects that interfere with viewing work, reading text, or finding contact information.

## Content and media

The home page needs a short introduction to the department and showcase, a clear route into the catalogue, and an industry contact route. Project pages must communicate the context, outcome, student contribution, and skills described in the project brief.

Plan for photography, graphic design, illustrations, film or animation, written work, and links to interactive projects. These are anticipated content types; the actual collection has not yet been supplied. The minimum version uses a representative image and links to further work. Multiple files, uploads, and video playback within the site are extensions.

Use concise descriptions and meaningful captions. Preserve media proportions and provide a way to see the complete work when thumbnails crop it. Clearly identify individual contributions to group projects. Publish only approved student details and media cleared for public use; use labelled sample content during development where needed.

## Accessibility and device experience

The minimum version must provide:

- Readable text, sufficient colour contrast, and information that does not rely on colour alone.
- Keyboard access, visible focus, meaningful headings, labelled forms, and clear validation messages.
- Alternative text for informative images. Provide captions or transcripts for included audiovisual work as appropriate.
- Usable layouts when zoomed, touch-friendly controls, and no essential actions available only on hover.
- Responsive media and navigation, with no horizontal page scrolling at typical mobile widths.

Desktop and mobile must support the same core tasks: browse, read a project, find a contact route, and manage entries as authorised staff. Mobile may stack content and collapse navigation or filters; desktop can use the additional space to show more work together. Students decide the layout through design and testing. More elaborate device-specific interactions are optional.

If adding motion or embedded video, respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid autoplay with sound. Optional accessibility controls do not replace an accessible default design.

## Inspiration

Use these references to discuss how student work is presented, then develop an original design:

- [Aalto ARTS Grad Show](https://www.aalto.fi/en/aalto-arts-handbook/aalto-arts-grad-show): a university showcase spanning architecture, design, film, art, and media. Consider how a shared identity can accommodate different disciplines.
- [UAL London College of Communication Graduate Showcase 2021](https://www.arts.ac.uk/colleges/london-college-of-communication/whats-on/degree-shows-and-showcase/graduate-showcase-2021): a graduate showcase reference. Consider how the introduction connects visitors to student work.

These are inspiration references, not required layouts or feature lists.

## Design feedback and client input

Students should present mobile and desktop wireframes for the main public journey and the lecturer publishing journey, followed by mock-ups and a working interface. Request lecturer/client feedback at each stage and record the resulting changes. Include feedback from a potential industry visitor where practical.

Client input still needed: approved branding and introductory copy, representative student work and credits, the initial categories, the industry contact destination, and confirmation of extension priorities. Record temporary assumptions in the project proposal while these details are being supplied.
