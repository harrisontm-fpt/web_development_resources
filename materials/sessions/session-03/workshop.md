# Session 03 workshop — Build a feasible backlog

[Lecture](lecture.md) · [Session index](../README.md)

**60-minute workshop + 60-minute project block · Ungraded**

## Before you start

Bring the real brief, board, and team agreement. Assign a facilitator, recorder, and scope challenger. Open the [proposal template](../../project_proposal.md).

## Step 1 — Identify users and journeys (0–15 minutes)

Read the minimum requirements. Identify the primary audience and write two or three important user journeys as short action sequences. Mark confirmed facts and assumptions.

Trace each journey to a brief requirement or a question for the client. Include relevant administrator/organiser tasks only when your project requires them.

## Step 2 — Write stories and finish conditions (15–35 minutes)

Choose the most important journey. Write a user story and at least three observable acceptance criteria, including one failure or empty-state condition where relevant.

Repeat for the next few essential outcomes. Put each story on the board. Split implementation tasks beneath or alongside it, retaining a link to the parent outcome. Add an owner and reviewer to work you intend to begin.

Swap one story with another team. Ask whether they could tell if it worked without inspecting your source code.

## Step 3 — Prioritise and estimate (35–50 minutes)

Label stories Must, Should, Could, or Won't this time. Compare every Must against client and assignment requirements so you do not omit deployment, testing, or documentation.

Estimate selected work in approximate hours with an uncertainty note. Add review/integration/testing tasks. Compare the total with your recorded availability and remove optional commitments until the plan is believable.

Write one weekly objective that can be demonstrated, not “work on the project.”

## Step 4 — Record limits and risks (50–60 minutes)

List at least three explicit exclusions or unconfirmed optional requests. Record the three most consequential risks/dependencies, each with an owner, next action, and follow-up date.

For one hypothetical new request, complete the scope-change table in the [management workbook](../../project_management.md). State what it would delay or replace.

## Evidence to keep

- [ ] Primary audience and essential journeys.
- [ ] Prioritised stories with observable acceptance criteria.
- [ ] Estimates, availability, and dependencies.
- [ ] Explicit exclusions.
- [ ] Risk/question owners and a follow-up date.
- [ ] Draft proposal linked to the board.

The suggested counts organise the exercise; they are not additional graded project features.

## Project block (60 minutes)

Use 5 minutes to set the immediate objective, 45 to develop proposal/backlog details, and 10 to record actions. Confirm the most consequential unknown with the client through your agreed communication process. Prepare an essential journey for wireframing next session.

**If blocked by an unanswered requirement:** write a provisional assumption and do work that remains useful under either answer. Do not represent the assumption as client agreement.
**If finished early:** select your largest task and split it into independently demonstrable steps.
**Exit question:** What will you defer first if your estimate proves optimistic?
